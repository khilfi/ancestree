Settings in fewer places, backups kept for 3 months, a simpler Export, downloads that say where they went, and adopted children drawn from their parents' marriage.

- **Settings in fewer places:** **Kinship** holds the kinship words and the relationship kinds, and **You and your family** holds Me, the families on this computer and the family folder. Each part keeps its heading, and **On this page** at the top takes you to it. Settings now reads Kinship, You and your family, What's missing, Import, Map, Backups, Trash, Help and About. Links to the old places still arrive at their part.
- **Backups kept for 3 months:** every backup, the daily ones and those you make, is kept for 3 months, then goes to your computer's bin (the Recycle Bin on Windows, the Bin on a Mac, the Trash on Linux), where you can still put it back. The newest 3 always stay, however old. A backup you add from elsewhere is kept for 3 months from when you add it. The copies in another place are kept the same way. To keep one longer, download it. **Settings → Backups** says what went to the bin, and when.
- **A simpler Export:** it offers a view-only copy and a picture of the tree. The full archive is a backup: make one with **Back up now** in **Settings → Backups**. The spreadsheet and GEDCOM are no longer offered, and view-only copies no longer carry them or the archive, so they're smaller. A copy's own Export offers the tree's picture and the import template.
- **Downloads that say where they went:** the import template, its example, a view-only copy, a backup and the tree's picture each say "Saved to Downloads: …", with **Show in folder**.
- **A fuller example spreadsheet:** **Example (.csv)** in **Settings → Import** is now the example family, every column filled in. The template gains **District**, for places with no postcode, and **Location**, where someone lives as a phone's map gives it, such as `3.139003, 101.686855`, which puts them on the map exactly there.
- **Adopted and foster children of a couple** now hang from the dot on their parents' marriage line, by one dashed line, as a couple's own children hang by a solid one. Parents who aren't a couple each keep a line of their own.
