Every country's map and places, addresses filled in as you type and pinned by themselves, life stories at the heart of AncesTree with what people say about them, a ready example family to look around, the fan chart unstuck, someone entered twice made one, and your own computers inviting.

- **Every country's map, and its places:** **Settings → Map** lists every country, those where your family lives or was born first, with a search. One **Download** brings a country's map close up and its places: every town, village and kampung, the estates, camps and hospitals people lived or were born at, and its postcodes, so they're found on the map. The biggest countries, the United States, Canada, China and Russia, also come one state at a time. The world's map now brings the world's towns of 500 people and its postcodes. If you had Malaysia, Singapore and Brunei, download Malaysia again: each country is its own download now.
- **Addresses, filled in as you type:** a place now has its address in one line, its district and its postcode. Type the address as you'd write it, or just a postcode, and AncesTree suggests the town, district, state and postcode; click one to fill them in. If the country's places aren't on your computer yet, it offers to download them.
- **At their parents':** someone with no place of their own is shown where their parents live, marked *(at their parents')*, on the map too. If the parents live apart, it's their mother's; their form lets you choose their father's, or **Lives elsewhere**. **Settings → What's missing** lists grown-ups shown that way, in case their own place was forgotten.
- **Pins that place themselves:** an address puts its person on the map by itself, with no internet, at its postcode's area or its town, and a small map in their form shows where. If it's wrong, drag the pin or click the right spot; or paste where it is, as numbers or a link from your phone's map. Everyone at one address shares one pin. When you download a country, its addresses move to better spots where AncesTree now knows them, never one you placed by hand. Where an address isn't found, you can **Look it up online once**: AncesTree says first what it sends, and to whom. **Settings → Map** lets you never be offered it.
- **Addresses stay yours:** GEDCOM and the spreadsheet leave addresses out unless you tick **Include addresses** in **Export**, and a copy that hides living people's details hides their addresses too. Every box in a person's form now grows as you type.
- **Life stories, at the heart of AncesTree:** **Life story**, beside **Edit** in someone's panel, or the small book mark on the photo of anyone with a story, opens it large, in the middle of the screen. **Write** has the parts of their life, each filled in only if you want: notable accomplishments; early, middle and later life; character and mannerisms; sayings and quotes; a message to the next generations; life values; hobbies; hidden talents; and free notes. **Read** lays it all out as a page of its own, with the line of their life and their family a click away, and **Print** prints it, or saves it as a PDF. It's still one file that opens in Notepad and Obsidian, and a view-only copy shows the same page. On a computer still on 0.7.0, the parts show in the story under their headings, and a story with what people say opens there as plain text until it updates.
- **What people say:** on a life story's page, anyone in the family can add their own words about someone, a relative whose computer can only view the family too. They're signed with your name under **Me** and the date. Only the computer that wrote them can change them or take them back, and the keeper can take any out. On a relative's computer they go to the keeper first, as other changes do; from a computer that can only view the family, they're all that goes.
- **A ready example family:** on a new computer, **Look around first** opens Keluarga Contoh, the example family: about 65 made-up people over six generations, with drawn portraits, life stories, what people say about them, and places from Kelantan to Singapore and Melbourne, so every view has something to show. It's a family of its own, never mixed with yours: open it any time from **Add a family → The example family**, and remove it as any other family. It can't join a family folder: that's for your own.
- **The fan chart and the hourglass stay put:** clicking someone opens their panel and leaves the chart as it is. To draw it from them, click **Fan from here** in their panel, or double-click them. **Back** and **Forward**, beside **Generations**, step through the people it was drawn from, and so do your mouse's back and forward buttons. In the fan chart, the person's children stand in a row beneath, so you can step down a generation too.
- **Someone entered twice:** Merge is now **Same person as…**, under **More** in their panel. AncesTree offers it where someone may be in the tree twice: on the message when you add someone who seems to be there already, and under **Possibly entered twice** in **Settings → What's missing**. **Undo** puts them back as two, photos and life stories too.
- **Invite and let in from another of your computers:** if you keep the family, turn on **May invite and let in for me** beside another computer of yours, in **Settings → Family folder**. From that computer you can then invite a relative and let their computer in once you've checked its code: the computer that keeps the family does it, within a minute while it runs. Both say so, and yours says when the keeper's computer isn't running. Removing, roles and reviewing changes stay on the keeper's computer.
- **Safer, after a review of everything new:** a relative's computer can no longer pass its words off as someone else's, yours included, nor make your computer stop with a story written to be slow to read. A computer you made a viewer is held to its words, whatever it says of when it sent them. A request to join that isn't as AncesTree writes it no longer stops your computer keeping in step, and your other computer's word to it is checked more closely. Words written on another computer show its name beside the writer's. A view-only copy that hides living people's details shows everyone's places to their town, those who have died too, and no longer says that their parents live apart. Words written with no **Me** chosen and no family folder are signed *Written on this computer*, never your computer's own name. Looking an address up online shows exactly what goes, and never follows Photon elsewhere. Downloads stop at the size they should be. And adding words to a story written in Notepad or Obsidian no longer makes an open **Write** say it changed outside AncesTree.

When you sign in to Google from AncesTree, Google says it hasn't verified the app: your family's Google project is made for one family, so it isn't. Choose **Advanced**, then **Go to AncesTree (unsafe)**.

**If you installed 0.1.0, 0.1.1 or 0.2.0**, install 0.8.0 from this page by hand: those versions don't know the key updates are signed with now, so they won't update themselves. 0.2.1 and later update themselves.
