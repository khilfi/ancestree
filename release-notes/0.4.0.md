Every family its own: each family's own Google project, several families on one computer, backups kept in a second place, and the family folder's state at the top.

**After updating, a family folder needs one step on each computer.** AncesTree no longer comes with a Google project of its own: each family signs in through its keeper's.

- **The keeper:** set up your family's Google project once, as [the guide](https://github.com/khilfi/ancestree/blob/main/GUIDE.md#your-familys-google-project) shows (about 20 minutes, free). Then in **Settings → Family folder**, click **Choose the client's file…**, and sign in again. Your family folder was made through the project AncesTree came with, which yours can't change: AncesTree offers **Rebuild the family folder**, which makes it again through your project, and relatives' computers follow it. Then send each relative the invitation (**Show the invitation**).
- **A relative:** in **Settings → Family folder**, paste the invitation your keeper sends you, and sign in again. Your family carries on as it was.

What's new:

- **Every family its own.** Whoever installs AncesTree keeps their own family, through their own Google project. Relatives join with an **invitation** the keeper sends: a line of text to paste into AncesTree. A laptop or Mac of the keeper's own joins with the keeper's own Google account.
- **Several families on one computer**, such as your mother's side and your father's, each kept completely apart: its own people, photos, backups and family folder. Click the family's name at the top to open another, or to add one, empty or from a backup. **Settings → Families** renames them, and removes one to AncesTree's bin for 30 days.
- **Keeping in step, shown.** At the top of the window, beside the family's name: **In step · 2 minutes ago**, **Offline**, or what's in the way. Click it to see when the family last arrived, or your last change went out, and what waits, with **Sync now**. The icon by the clock says the same, and its menu has **Sync now**. AncesTree now keeps in step a few seconds after each change, and once more before it closes.
- **Room at the top.** To fit the family's name and how it stands, **Export**, **Dictionary** and **Settings** show only their icons unless the window is wide. Point at one to see its name.
- **Copies of your backups in another place.** **Settings → Backups → Copies in another place**: a USB stick, another disk, or a folder OneDrive or Dropbox keeps, locked with a password if you like. A locked copy opens with its password, and the double tick beside a backup checks it, restoring nothing.
- **The family folder made again.** If it's ever lost from Google Drive, the keeper's computer rebuilds it, with **Rebuild the family folder**, and relatives' computers follow it by themselves. The keeper can also move it to another Google account or project.
- **A first start that could never finish**, on about one computer in 64, is put right: Neo4j's tool took the database's new password for an instruction. A computer stuck that way starts at once with this version.
- **Backups know their family**, so one family's can't be restored into another by mistake.

When you sign in to Google from AncesTree, Google says it hasn't verified the app: your family's Google project is made for one family, so it isn't. Choose **Advanced**, then **Go to AncesTree (unsafe)**.

**If you installed 0.1.0, 0.1.1 or 0.2.0**, install 0.4.0 from this page by hand: those versions don't know the key updates are signed with now, so they won't update themselves. 0.2.1 and later update themselves.
