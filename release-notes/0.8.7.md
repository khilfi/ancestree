AncesTree keeps your family's data whole and its facts right, and when it's removed from a computer, the backups stay safe.

- **Removing AncesTree, with the backups safe:** on Windows, the uninstaller now says what it deletes. Deleting the families used to delete their backups too, without saying so; now **But keep the backups** moves each family's to Documents\AncesTree backups first, those of a family removed in the last 30 days too, unless you untick it. Copies kept on a USB stick or in OneDrive are never touched.
- **A warning on the keeper's computer:** the uninstaller says what keeping the family again needs (your recovery code, your Google project's client file and the keeper's Google account), and whether a backup was copied to another place in the last 7 days.
- **Remove AncesTree from this computer…,** in **Settings → About**, on Windows, the Mac and Linux: it says what goes and what stays, saves the backups to any folder, asks the keeper for their three things (with **Save it again…** for the client file) or to hand the family over, and sends what waits for the keeper. On a Mac or Linux, AncesTree's folder goes to the Bin, where it can still be taken out.
- **A loop of parents, or a child with three birth parents:** a family that recorded someone as their own ancestor, or gave a child three birth parents, which an older AncesTree could store, no longer stops the tree opening, or a relative's computer taking the family in once it's put right. **Settings → What's missing** lists each under **A loop of parents** or **A child with three birth parents**, with the link to take out.
- **Back from the Trash:** someone restored comes back with every link the tree's rules still allow, each as it was, a marriage's order too. A link the rules now refuse stays out, and AncesTree says which.
- **Undo, whole or not at all:** Undo of a merge works again when the merge left a link with the other person. An undo that can't be done whole isn't done at all, and says why. A country's places arriving no longer clears Redo.
- **Relatives' computers:** a date or a place changed on a relative's computer stays whole when the keeper's family moves on, never a date nobody entered, such as 31 February. What still can't come in is as the keeper has it, and is said.
- **Restoring a backup:** a backup whose family doesn't hang together (a date that can't be read, a loop of parents, a child with three birth parents) is refused, saying what's wrong, before anything is replaced.
- **Elder and younger:** a date that's only *about*, *before* or *between* no longer makes someone elder or younger for certain where it could be either. The words say both, such as *pakdhé / paklik*, until an order is set.
- **A birth order you set stays** when a child is added later. The new child takes their place by date, or comes last.
- **How two people are related** reads the same whichever of them you start from. Two ways that say it equally well are both shown under **Also related as…**.
- **A husband's other wife** is a *co-wife* (*madu*) only when both were married to him at the same time. Otherwise she is *her husband's other wife* (*isteri lain suami*), or *her late husband's other wife* (*isteri lain arwah suami*).
- **Someone with no place of their own** is shown only at a living parent's by birth or adoption, never a late parent's or a foster parent's. When the parents live apart, they're shown with the parent they sit under on the tree.
- **Merging someone entered twice** keeps the order of their marriages and counts birth parents by gender, so a second birth father stays with the other.
- **An unknown parent** goes to the Trash with the last of their children, and comes back with them.
- **Spreadsheets:** decades such as *1950s* are read as dates. Places such as *Pattani* or *Taman Melati, Gombak* are read for what they are, and *Melaka* alone is the town and its state. A postcode Excel took the leading zero from isn't a change.
- **The same address in two states** is no longer one household.
- **Your own other computers:** whether another of your computers invited someone before or after you removed their computer is now told by the family's record, not by the computers' clocks. Update the keeper's computer first: until it has 0.8.7, it turns down what an updated computer asks.
- **Errors** are said in plain words, never a computer's codes.
