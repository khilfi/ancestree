A calmer, clearer AncesTree.

- **Checked in a browser before each release:** the app's main journeys, from a new install's welcome to Export and Esc in every dialog, now run in a browser at two window sizes, with pictures compared, so a change to one screen can't break another unseen.
- **One warm look:** every button, field and highlight now comes from one warm colour set: no more black buttons on a warm page.
- **Easier to read:** no text is smaller than 12 pixels, the map's names included, and pale grey text is darker, so it reads clearly.
- **By keyboard:** press Tab to go from button to button. The one you're on has a blue ring, and shows the same hint as pointing at it.
- **The top bar:** Tree, Timeline, Map, Add person and Search people keep their words in smaller windows. Export, the Dictionary, the Guide and Settings are in **More**, each with its words, and the Dictionary has its own icon.
- **One toolbar:** the tree's, the timeline's and the map's toolbars look and size alike.
- **Steadier loading:** shapes stand where the family will appear, instead of "Loading…".
- **Esc closes what's on top:** a box first, then a card or a panel such as Family facts, then someone's panel, one at each press. While finding a relationship, Esc ends that first. In the person form, Esc is Cancel.
- **Nothing typed is lost without a question:** in the person form, in Add a relative and in a relationship's kind, once you've typed something, Cancel, Esc and × ask "Discard your changes?" first; Esc there keeps editing.
- **Back where you were:** as a box or card closes, you're back on the button that opened it. The card after dragging a link has its own close button.
- **One note for each change,** with anything worth a second look inside it and its Undo always in view.
- **Plain words when something fails:** "AncesTree's engine isn't answering." with Try again, never "Failed to fetch".
- **Someone's panel, its actions at the top:** under their name, **Edit**, **Life story** and **More**, which holds Find relationship, Centre the tree here, Same person as… and Move to Trash, no longer at the bottom after every detail.
- **Save and Cancel always in view:** they stay at the form's foot, however far down you are, and **Ctrl+Enter** (⌘+Enter on a Mac) saves from any box.
- **Dates typed as they're said:** one box reads "12/3/1950" (the day first), "March 1950", "about 1950", "before 1960", "1950–1955", "kira-kira 1950" or "tahun 1950-an", and says beside it what it understood. What it can't read is said once you leave the box. The lists are still there, behind the calendar.
- **A family added faster:** **+ Parent**, **+ Spouse** and **+ Child** sit just below the person chosen on the tree. **Add and add another** empties the form for the next one: after a father it offers a mother, and the next child keeps the other parent.
- **The person you chose stays in view:** as their panel opens or widens, or when someone is added out of sight, the tree moves just enough to keep them clear of it. The panel opens as wide as you left it.
- **Short years as sure as their dates:** someone born "about 1920" or "before 1950" showed as "b. 1920" or "b. 1950", as if certain. Wherever years are shown short, on the tree, in the relatives, on the timeline and in search, they now say so: "b. c. 1920", "b. before 1950", "b. 1976–1978".
- **Words that agree:** the form's "Where they rest" is **Buried at**, as the details say it; someone who has died "lived in" a place; and the life story's line reads in sentences, "Married …", "Died 2011".
- **Undo of adding someone** closes their panel, rather than leaving it to say "That person isn't in the tree."
- **The map opens on the whole family:** every place they live is in view at once. Click a country's or a state's circle to zoom in to its people, in any size of window.
- **Names you can read on the map:** no name covers another, a circle, or the map's buttons and credits. Singapore and Brunei list their towns, not "State not known".
- **More room for the map:** on a narrow window, the list of places steps aside when someone's panel opens.
- **Faces, not empty rings:** until someone's photo has arrived, their initials show.
- **The timeline scrolls as you'd expect:** the mouse wheel goes down the list; hold Ctrl, or pinch, to zoom.
- **The timeline's colours explained:** a key says what each colour means, and everyone is named as on the tree, with their nickname after.
- **One name for each thing:** the person panel's tab is **Life story**, as its button is; **Family facts** is Family facts everywhere; a family removed goes to the Trash, as a person does.
- **Each thing in one place:** Family facts opens from the ⓘ beside the family's name, at any window width, and **What's missing** is its second tab, no longer in Settings. **Me** chooses who you are, and now forgets it too. The language of kinship words is chosen beside a relationship's answer and in the Dictionary; Settings keeps the Malay titles. Settings' **Help** opens the guide.
- **Plainer choices:** "Centre" means one thing, **Centre the tree here**, with **Use the oldest ancestor** to go back. **Show everyone**, or **F**, shows everything on the tree, the timeline and the map. **Export** is a short menu: **View-only copy…** and **Picture of the tree**.
- **Married-in families** are placed as **Fans** or **Tucked in**; a computer that chose Discs or Settled fans shows fans.
- **The tree, readable:** it opens close enough to read every name, around the oldest ancestors, rather than everyone as dots; **Show everyone** shows everyone. Names sit on a soft background, so lines pass behind them, never across, and the lines from families who married in go around names.
- **The tree's tools above it:** in one line above the tree, not over the people, with zooming and **Show everyone** at the end; in a narrow window, the rest wait under ⋯. The small map of the whole tree is smaller, at the bottom left where opening someone's panel doesn't move it, and can be hidden.
- **Ring labels:** each generation is named once, on the family's own rings; a family who married in has its first ring named for its couple, such as *Rashid & Kamariah*. The tree's picture shows names on the same soft background.
- **With the keyboard:** Tab goes from person to person on the tree, showing each one's card with their full name; Enter opens them.
